I'm a Computer Science graduate from the **University of Bath**, where I completed an MComp (Hons) with First Class honours. My work sits at the point where **machine learning meets symbolic computation** — teaching models to make the decisions that computer algebra systems have traditionally left to hand-written heuristics.

That thread runs through both of my papers. At [ICMS 2024](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-031-64529-7_20) I looked at graph neural network representations for choosing variable orderings in cylindrical algebraic decomposition, and found that the standard benchmark datasets were badly polluted in ways that flattered existing results. More recently, at SCML 2026, I replaced the heuristic rule ordering inside **SymPy**'s symbolic integrator with a learned policy, trained with reinforcement learning rather than from a pre-labelled dataset.

Alongside the research I spent a year at **SLB Cambridge Research**, where I finetuned and deployed an LLM-based system for well safety (now under invention disclosure), adapted the Kuhn–Munkres and Murty algorithms for rig scheduling, and built a digital twin of a solar energy storage system to optimise cost and emissions. Before that I was at the **University of Exeter**, working on Bayesian imputation and Transformer-based forecasting for noisy weather data.

I'm interested in reinforcement learning, computer algebra, combinatorial optimisation, and getting language models to do useful work on structured problems. You can find my [publications](/publications/) and [CV](/cv/) here, or reach me by email.
